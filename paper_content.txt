ABSTRACT
Video content providers are increasingly interested in inter-
active live streaming since user engagement increases their
revenues. To provide high quality of experience (QoE), it is
critical to design a low delay adaptive bitrate (ABR) algo-
rithm, but which is lacked in existing studies. The low delay
constraint poses much more challenges to achieve high bi-
trate and low rebuffering. For example, low delay requires the
player to maintain a small playback buffer, which, however,
increases the risk of rebuffering. This work designs a low
delay ABR algorithm called HCA which provides good QoE
by controlling the buffer occupancy at a low but non-empty
level. To achieve accurate control, HCA uses the hybrid of
feedback and feedforward control to regulate the buffer dy-
namic (buffer occupancy and its variation) based on predic-
tions of future network condition (throughput and its varia-
tion). Trace-driven experiments show that HCA achieves zero
rebuffering for 98% of all traces while ensuring high bitrate.
Index T erms —HTTP adaptive bitrate streaming, low de-
lay, interactive live streaming
1. INTRODUCTION
Since user engagement increases revenue, video content
providers like Y ouTube and Twitter’s Periscope are increas-
ingly interested in interactive live streaming where viewers
can interact with the broadcaster when watching video [1].
To maintain interactivity, live streaming requires the time be-
tween the event is recorded and is viewed (i.e., the delay) is
low. Content providers strive to provide good quality of expe-
rience (QoE) under low delay constraint to attract more users.
HTTP Adaptive Bitrate (HAS) streaming (standardized
as DASH [2]) is the primary tool to optimize QoE in vary-
ing network conditions. It splits a video into chunks with
each encoded into several bitrates, and uses client-side adap-
tive bitrate (ABR) algorithm to dynamically adjust bitrate in
the unit of chunk. Commercial players such as Netﬂix and
Y ouTube use HAS to improve QoE [3]. To better support in-
teractive streaming, HAS recently added low delay option to
allow chunk to be played before completely downloaded.Despite the broad interest in the design of ABR algorithm,
existing works mainly focus on V oD streaming which has no
constraint of low delay. What is critically lacking is a low de-
lay algorithm for interactive live streaming. However, select-
ing the right bitrate with low delay constraint is much more
challenging due to (1) the conﬂicts between low delay and lit-
tle rebuffering (low delay requires small playback buffer oc-
cupancy while continuous playback need large buffering to
absorb the impact of throughput ﬂuctuations); (2) the difﬁ-
culty of delivering high-quality video while without inducing
rebuffering and increasing the delay (videos of higher bitrate
requires longer time to download, which might drain out the
buffer thus inducing rebuffering and increasing the delay).
Contributions. To provide good experience of both view-
ing and interactivity, this work designs a low delay ABR al-
gorithm (called as HCA) based on Hybrid Control, which em-
ploys a small buffer capacity (i.e., maximum buffer size) and
selects the highest safe bitrate while preventing the buffer oc-
cupancy from empty within that constraint. Since the buffer
dynamic reﬂects the mismatch between bitrate and through-
put, HCA adjusts bitrate by controlling the buffer occupancy
at a low but non-empty level, thus achieving bitrate maxi-
mization and rebuffering minimization at the same time.
To accurately control the buffer occupancy in unstable
networks, HCA borrows from the design of V oD ABRs. Ex-
isting adaptations are mainly based on feedforward which
maximizes QoE using throughput predictions [4, 5], or based
on feedback which relies on buffer occupancy without predic-
tion [6, 7]. Feedforward can adapt to future network changes
but the performance is sensitive to prediction error, whereas
the adaptation of feedback is robust against throughput ﬂuctu-
ations but is reactive. Inspired by this, HCA achieves accurate
control by using a hybrid control which makes rapid reaction
to throughput changes by feedforward control and resists the
impact of prediction error by feedback control.
In detail, HCA designs a reference buffer dynamic tra-
jectory with the points on which evolving towards the target
level. The buffer occupancy is controlled to follow the trajec-
tory to move to the target. By using hybrid control, HCA
ﬁrst computes the bitrate that needed to reach the desired

2. BACKGROUND AND MOTIV ATION
Overview of HAS: HAS is currently the most popular
technology for streaming V oD and live videos. It can not only
stream cost-effectively to a large audience by using CDNs
and HTTP servers, but also ensure good QoE by adapting bi-
trate to varying bandwidth. Live streaming requires low de-
lay but V oD streaming has no requirement. To better support
live streaming, HAS provides low delay option by leverag-
ing HTTP chunked transfer, which delivers chunk in unit of
smaller segment so that the chunk can be played before it is
completely downloaded [1]. The core of HAS is ABR algo-
rithm, which heavily inﬂuences the achieved QoE. ABR se-
lects bitrate based on throughput predictions and buffer occu-
pancy with the goal of optimizing QoE, including maximizing
bitrate and minimizing rebuffering [6, 7].
State-of-the-art ABR algorithms: Existing ABRs are
mainly designed for V oD streaming, which can be roughly di-
vided into feedback-based and feedforward-based algorithms.
As shown in Figure 1(a), feedback-based ABRs [6, 7] ad-
just bitrate to reduce the offset between the current buffer
level and the target. By continuously taking corrective ac-
tions, feedback is robust against throughput ﬂuctuations.
However, it is also reactive. Without making prediction, the
corrective action is taken only after there is a buffer offset.
Feedforward-based approach [4, 8, 5] predicts future through-
puts and selects the bitrate that matches the throughput or out-
puts the maximum QoE. The idea is illustrated in Figure 1(b).

ABRs are able to make rapid reaction to network changes.
However, the performance is sensitive to prediction error.
Both of the two methodologies might induce signiﬁcant
buffer occupancy variation in rapidly changing networks.
However, they can provide good QoE for V oD streamings
because the impact of ﬂuctuations can be absorbed by large
buffer occupancy so as to not lead to rebuffering.
Challenges of designing a low delay ABR: Due to the
ﬂuctuating network throughput and the conﬂicting QoE opti-
mization goals, picking a correct bitrate is hard for both V oD
streaming and live streaming. However, the stringent low de-
lay requirement poses more challenges for live streaming to
provide good QoE. The new challenges are (1) low delay re-
quires small playback buffer occupancy, but which impairs
the resistance to throughput ﬂuctuations and increases the risk
of rebuffering. (2) increasing video quality will increase the
risk of rebuffering and long delay, because minimizing re-
buffering and delay would constantly select the lowest bitrate.
Motivation and basic idea: To achieve both good QoE
and low delay, ABR algorithm should use a small buffer ca-
pacity and under this limitation to select the highest safe bi-
trate without exhausting the buffer. Since the buffer occu-
pancy increases at the speed of network throughput and de-
creases at the speed of video bitrate, the selected bitrate fully
utilizes the network capacity if the buffer occupancy is con-
stant. Considering that the buffer dynamic not only reﬂects
the bandwidth utilization but also relates to rebuffering, the
QoE optimization of low delay streaming can be solved by
controlling the buffer occupancy at a low but non-empty level.
The key to realizing this potentiality is accurately controlling
the buffer occupancy. Motivated by the analysis of existing
V oD ABRs, we propose to use the hybrid of feedback con-
trol and feedforward control to achieve accurate control. The
feedforward controller is used to eliminate the effect of large
throughput variation by taking a corrective action ahead of
time, and the feedback controller focuses on dealing with the
prediction error. By using hybrid control, we can decouple the
response to throughput variations and the resistance to distur-
bances, achieving both robustness and quick response.

4. HYBRID CONTROL-BASED ABR
With the formulation in Section 3, bitrate is selected by
controlling (Xk,Yk)at(0,0). To achieve accurate control, we
regulates the evolution of (Xk,Yk)not just the instantaneous
state of(Xk,Yk). HCA designs a trajectory that evolves to-
wards the origin and controls the system state to move along
it. Figure 3 illustrates the framework of HCA. After design-
ing a desired trajectory Sk=f(Xk,Yk), there are four steps
to determine the bitrate Rkfor chunk k:
(1) Predict Throughput: Predict the throughput ˆTkand the
range of error ξk∈[ˆξmin,ˆξmax].(2) Generate feedforward signal: Given current state
(Xk,Yk), compute the signal ΔRffneeded to output the de-
sired behavior Sk+1=f(Xk+1,Yk+1)=0 based on the
system model in Eq.(2) and the throughput prediction ˆTk.
(3) Generate feedback signal: ΔRfdis set as a function
of current state (Xk,Yk)to decrease the current distance to
the desired trajectory in the condition of ξk∈[ˆξmin,ˆξmax].
(4) Output bitrate decision: With the two control signals
combined, the requested video bitrate Rkis the highest avail-
able bitrate lower than Rk−1+ΔRff+ΔRfd.

5. EV ALUATION
To evaluate HCA, we carry out experiments on a reference
DASH player using realistic network traces.
5.1. Experimental Setup and Adaptation Methods
Throughput traces: We use an HSDPA mobile dataset
[11] and a proprietary dataset from KwaiShou [12], which isa leading commercial video provider in China. The HSDPA
dataset comprises measurements of video streaming through-
put of a moving device in Telenor’s 3G/HSDPA mobile wire-
less network in Norway. For evaluation experiments, we gen-
erate 300 traces with 300 continuous 1s throughput measure-
ments from randomly selected traces for each dataset.
Implementation and setup: We use Nginx as the basis
of server deployment and Dash.js as the video player [13]. To
implement algorithms, the original Dash.js is modiﬁed using
the methods in [4]. Our evaluation uses video “Envivio” from
[14] which consists of 100 2s chunks with each encoded in
{0.35, 0.6, 1, 2, 3 }Mbps. The buffer capacity is Qmax=5s.
ABR algorithms: HCA selects bitrate by Eq.(7) with
Qr=2s,λ=0.75andαbounded by Eq.(8). Throughput
predictor adopts the setting of Ω=1 05andμ=0.7.F o r
comparison, several typical V oD algorithms are evaluated:
Feedback-based approaches:
(1) BBA [6]: selects bitrate by a function of buffer occupancy
with a reservoir of 2s and a cushion of 3s. The goal is to
control the buffer occupancy between 2s and 5s.
(2) BOLA [7]: is designed based on Lyapunov optimization,
the bitrate decision of which is essentially a function of cur-
rent buffer occupancy.
Feedforward-based approaches:
(3) MPC [4]: maximizes the designed QoE utility over 5
chunks with throughput predictions.
(4) RobustMPC (denoted as RMPC) [4]: uses the same ap-
proach as MPC but mitigates the effect of prediction error by
normalizing throughput predictions with the maximum pre-
diction error observed in the past 5 chunks.
(5) RB: picks the highest bitrate less than the estimated
throughput of the next chunk.
QoE metrics: (a) average bitrate; (b) total rebuffer-
ing time; (c) QoE utility, computed by QoE=Rate−
γ×RebufTime . Since rebuffering not only degrades QoE
but also increases the delay, so we adopt the “Avoid Rebuffer-
ing” setting, i.e., γ=6, as recommended in [4].

7. CONCLUSION
In this work, we present a low-delay bitrate adaptation so-
lution HCA, which optimizes QoE with the limitation of small
buffer by controlling the buffer occupancy at a small but non-
empty level. To accurately control the buffer occupancy in
unstable network, HCA uses the hybrid of feedback control
and feedforward control, achieving both robustness and rapid
reaction. Over a large throughput dataset, evaluation exper-
iments demonstrate that HCA achieves zero rebuffering for
98% of all traces while providing high bitrate.
